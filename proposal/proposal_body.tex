\begin{quote}
\small\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\textbf{Abstract.}
Online controllers in evolving Markov decision processes (MDPs) choose between acting on a fast low-fidelity surrogate ($L$) and running slower high-fidelity simulation batches ($H$) whose runtime introduces information age or actuation delay. Accurate \emph{marginal} cost predictions are insufficient for decision-making in general \citep{wen2021predictions}; we identify which cross-age and cross-action discrepancy covariance entries determine whether high-fidelity verification pays net of delay, and test whether they matter in urban traffic signal control. Statistically, we propose a masked, heteroscedastic hierarchical Gaussian double-difference model fitted via blocked Gibbs sampling and full-covariance structured variational inference (SVI), separating structural discrepancy covariance from common-random-number simulation noise while handling exact structural zeros induced by signal-legality rules. Theoretically, in a two-alternative setting, we characterize exact over- and under-computation conditions for marginal-matched factorized beliefs, state the tie-safe excess-risk decomposition, and target perturbation and queue-memory decorrelation bounds. Empirically, motivated by pilot SUMO diagnostics showing $+1.53\%$ closed-loop gain from precomputed $60\,\mathrm{s}$ verification over \texttt{greedy5}, we design a pre-registered information-age gate and 72-date benchmark on 2023 RESCO v2.0 Salt Lake City workloads.
\end{quote}

\section{Introduction and Related Literature}

Real-time controllers in traffic signal control, robotics, and dynamic dispatch compute while the physical system evolves. A fast queueing surrogate ($L$) scores candidate actions in milliseconds but incurs approximation bias, whereas high-fidelity microsimulation ($H$) improves evaluation accuracy at the cost of wall-clock deliberation. Under \emph{concurrent precomputation}, running a heavier simulation batch requires starting earlier from a staler state $S_{t_0 - L}$ of information age $L$; under \emph{post-epoch deliberation}, waiting $\tau$ while a fallback policy $\pi_0$ operates incurs staleness and may forfeit candidate phases to legality constraints. We ask: \emph{when does multi-fidelity simulation verification pay net of information age or actuation delay, and which entries of the joint discrepancy covariance govern that decision?}

\textbf{Related literature.}
Metareasoning treats deliberation as costly \citep{russell1991principles,hansen2001monitoring}; thinking lets the environment drift \citep{lin2015metareasoning}, as in real-time and execution-delayed MDPs \citep{ramstedt2019realtime,derman2021delayed} and delay-compensated MPC \citep{findeisen2004delay}, and computation budgets can be learned via RL \citep{magnino2026learning,muppidi2026time}. Those works use a single simulator fidelity with learned or myopic Value-of-Computation ($\VoC$) surrogates. Conversely, Bayesian value of information for simulations \citep{hay2012selecting}, multi-level simulator discrepancy models \citep{kennedy2000predicting,kennedy2001bayesian}, multi-fidelity Knowledge Gradient (\texttt{misoKG}; \citealp{poloczek2017miso}), and correlated KG with common random numbers \citep{frazier2009knowledge,xie2016pairwise} assume a static target objective during sampling, while economic sampling models price response delay on fixed arms \citep{chick2012sequential,chick2017delayed}. Whereas \citet{wen2021predictions} establish generally that joint predictions matter beyond marginals, our focus is the multi-fidelity regime where simulation evidence collected on an earlier state informs action costs at a \emph{later} execution state, bridged by queueing metamodels \citep{osorio2013simulation} and online traffic microsimulation \citep{goodall2013traffic}.

\section{Problem Formulation: Information Age and Actuation Latency}

Consider a finite-horizon MDP $M = (\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, P, c, T)$ with control epochs on a grid (e.g., $5\,\mathrm{s}$ intervals) and physical evaluation horizon $T$ ($T \ge 60\,\mathrm{s}$). We formalize two timing regimes under a common reactive baseline $\pi_0$:
\[
\text{\textbf{(Precomputed / Information Age $L$):}}\quad
\underbrace{S_{t_0 - L}}_{\text{observe } x,\, \text{launch } H}
\xrightarrow[\text{prev.\ action runs}]{\text{compute budget } \le L}
\underbrace{S_{t_0}}_{\text{apply legal } a}
\xrightarrow{\text{shared continuation}}
[t_0, t_0 + T),
\]
where computation for epoch $t_0$ runs concurrently over $[t_0 - L, t_0)$ from causal features $x = \phi(S_{t_0 - L})$, and larger simulation budgets or multi-intersection sharing require larger lead age $L > 0$. By contrast, under \emph{off-grid post-epoch delay} $\tau > 0$ ($\pi_0$ acts at $t_0$ and $a$ is requested at $t_0 + \tau$), phase switches by $\pi_0$ at $t_0$ can deterministically forfeit $a$ under minimum-green rules; moreover, because $L$ is nearly free, re-evaluating $L$ on fresh state $S_{t_0}$ or $S_{t_0 + \tau}$ (\texttt{refresh-L}) weakly dominates acting on stale $L$ alone and serves as either a substitute or complement to $H$-verification.

\textbf{Multi-fidelity costs, structural zeros, and discrepancy.}
Indexing age/delay by $\ell \ge 0$, let $\pi_{a,\ell}$ denote the candidate policy evaluated over horizon $T$ with shared continuation (\texttt{greedy5}) and matched exogenous demand seed $\xi_r$. Relative to baseline $\pi_0$ ($g^F_i(\pi_0)=0$), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discrepancy-def}
g^F_i(a,\ell) \coloneqq \E_\xi\!\left[C^F(\pi_{a,\ell}; s_i, \xi) - C^F(\pi_0; s_i, \xi) \,\middle|\, s_i\right], \quad F \in \{L, H\},
\qquad
\delta_i(a,\ell) \coloneqq g^H_i(a,\ell) - g^L_i(a,\ell).
\end{equation}
Crucially, whenever signal timers and $\pi_0$'s deterministic rule force $\pi_{a,\ell} \equiv \pi_0$, both $C^H - C^H_0$ and $C^L - C^L_0$ vanish pathwise, yielding \emph{structural zeros} $\delta_i(a,\ell) \equiv 0$ known ex-ante at decision time.

\section{Masked Heteroscedastic Model and Value-of-Computation Rule}

\subsection{Paired Double-Difference Model with Structural Masking}

Across $n$ saved states ($i=1,\dots,n$), $R_i \ge 2$ conditionally independent paired scenario replicates ($r=1,\dots,R_i$), and a grid of $K$ candidate--age pairs $j = (a_j, \ell_j) \in \{1,\dots,K\}$, we observe double-differences
\begin{equation}\label{eq:double-diff}
y_{irj} = \big(C^H_{irj} - C^H_{ir0}\big) - \big(C^L_{irj} - C^L_{ir0}\big).
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal{M}_i \subseteq \{1,\dots,K\}$ denote the ex-ante computable mask of \emph{active} coordinates where $\pi_{a_j,\ell_j} \not\equiv \pi_0$ (setting $\delta_{ij} = y_{irj} = 0$ on $\mathcal{M}_i^c$), and let $P_i \in \{0,1\}^{|\mathcal{M}_i| \times K}$ extract $\mathcal{M}_i$. To avoid both the $K(K+1)/2$ parameter blow-up of unstructured covariances and the asymptotic collapse of homoscedastic thresholds as $n \to \infty$, we model state-dependent marginal scales $\Sigma(x_i) = D(x_i) R D(x_i)$ with $D(x_i) = \diag(\exp(Z_i \gamma))$ and structured correlation $R = R_{\mathcal{A}} \otimes R_{\mathcal{L}}$ (using an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck / VAR temporal kernel $[R_{\mathcal{L}}]_{uv} = \exp(-|\ell_u - \ell_v|/\vartheta)$ rather than linear delay interpolation, which overstates mid-grid temporal correlation):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hier-model}
\boldsymbol\delta_i \mid \beta, \gamma, R \stackrel{\mathrm{ind}}{\sim} \Normal\!\big(X_i\beta,\, \Sigma(x_i)\big),
\qquad
\mathbf{y}_{ir,\mathcal{M}_i} \mid \boldsymbol\delta_i, \Omega \stackrel{\mathrm{ind}}{\sim} \Normal\!\big(P_i \boldsymbol\delta_i,\, P_i \Omega P_i^{\mathsf T}\big).
\end{equation}
Replication $R_i \ge 2$ identifies paired common-random-number noise $\Omega$ from within-state differences while cross-state variation identifies $\Sigma(x_i)$. Because standard $\IW(\nu, cI)$ priors shrink correlations toward zero (biasing toward factorized beliefs), we place separated priors on log-scales $\gamma$, regression weights $\beta \sim \Normal(m_0, V_0)$, and correlation parameters $(R_{\mathcal{A}}, \vartheta)$.

\subsection{Inference and Online Value-of-Computation ($\VoC$) Rule}

We pair blocked MCMC / Gibbs updates with \emph{full-covariance structured variational inference} (SVI), retaining a dense $|\mathcal{M}_i| \times |\mathcal{M}_i|$ covariance in $q(\boldsymbol\delta_{i,\mathcal{M}_i})$ so coordinate-wise mean-field factorization does not sever cross-action or cross-age dependence. At test state $x_\star$, given predictive belief $P$ over $\mathbf{g}^H_\star = \mathbf{g}^L_\star + \boldsymbol\delta_\star$ and query observation $\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{\star,Q}$ (incorporating \texttt{refresh-L} updates when fresh $L$ is observed at actuation), let $V_{\mathrm{now}}(x_\star) = \min_{a \in \mathcal{A}_0} \E_P[g^H_\star(a,0)]$, $V_{\mathrm{stale}}(x_\star) = \min_{a \in \mathcal{A}_0} \E_P[g^H_\star(a,\ell)]$, and $V_{\mathrm{ver}}(x_\star; P) = \E_P[\min_{a \in \mathcal{A}_0} \E_P[g^H_\star(a,\ell) \mid \bar{\mathbf{y}}_{\star,Q}]]$. Net Value of Computation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:voc-decomp}
\VoC(x_\star; P) \coloneqq V_{\mathrm{now}}(x_\star) - V_{\mathrm{ver}}(x_\star; P)
= \underbrace{\big(V_{\mathrm{stale}}(x_\star) - V_{\mathrm{ver}}(x_\star; P)\big)}_{\text{Information Gain } \IG(x_\star; P)\,\ge\,0}
\;-\;
\underbrace{\big(V_{\mathrm{stale}}(x_\star) - V_{\mathrm{now}}(x_\star)\big)}_{\text{Age / Staleness Penalty } \Delta_{\mathrm{delay}}(x_\star)}.
\end{equation}
When $|\mathcal{A}_0| \ge 3$ (e.g., $\{a_1, a_2, \pi_0\}$), $\IG(x_\star; P)$ is evaluated via Monte Carlo or Clark's approximation; when $\Delta_{\mathrm{delay}}(x_\star) \le 0$ and $\IG(x_\star; P) > 0$, verification is unconditionally optimal.

\section{Theoretical Analysis: Joint vs.\ Marginal-Matched Beliefs}

\subsection{Two-Alternative Threshold and Exact Covariance Conditions}

Fix state $x$, a \emph{two-alternative} active choice set $\mathcal{A}_0 = \{a_1, a_2\}$ ($|\mathcal{A}_0|=2$), age/delay $\ell > 0$, and batch size $B$ on query set $Q$ over $\boldsymbol\delta = (\delta_{1,0}, \delta_{2,0}, \delta_{1,\ell}, \delta_{2,\ell})^{\mathsf T}$. Compare true joint belief $P_{\mathrm{joint}}: \boldsymbol\delta \sim \Normal(\boldsymbol\mu, \Sigma)$ against the \emph{marginal-matched factorized belief} $P_{\mathrm{fact}}: \boldsymbol\delta \sim \Normal(\boldsymbol\mu, \diag(\Sigma))$, while retaining the shared $-\varepsilon_{r0}$ baseline noise covariance in $R_B(P_{\mathrm{fact}})$ where applicable. Both beliefs share identical $a^\star_0$, $V_{\mathrm{now}}$, $\Delta_{\mathrm{delay}}$, and prior delayed contrast $m_\ell \coloneqq \mu^H_{1,\ell} - \mu^H_{2,\ell}$. With $\mathbf{c}_\ell = (0,0,1,-1)^{\mathsf T}$, gain vector $\mathbf{w}(P) = (C_{Q,Q}(P) + R_B(P))^{-1} C_{Q,\delta}(P)\mathbf{c}_\ell$, and posterior-mean contrast variance $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}^2(P) \coloneqq \mathbf{c}_\ell^{\mathsf T} C_{\delta,Q}(P)\mathbf{w}(P)$, $|\mathcal{A}_0|=2$ admits the exact closed form \citep{frazier2009knowledge}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed-voc}
\VoC(P) = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}(P)\,\psi\!\left(\frac{|m_\ell|}{\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}(P)}\right) - \Delta_{\mathrm{delay}},
\qquad
\psi(z) \coloneqq \varphi(z) - z\big(1 - \Phi(z)\big),
\qquad
\frac{d}{d\sigma}\!\left[\sigma\psi\!\left(\frac{|m_\ell|}{\sigma}\right)\right] = \varphi\!\left(\frac{|m_\ell|}{\sigma}\right) > 0.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Cross-Age and Cross-Action Threshold Shifts]\label{lem:regimes}
For $|\mathcal{A}_0|=2$ with matched pre-query marginals:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Cross-age transfer:} On initial-age queries $Q \subseteq \{(1,0),(2,0)\}$, independence gives $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}(P_{\mathrm{fact}}) = 0$, incurring stopping regret $(\IG(P_{\mathrm{joint}}) - \Delta_{\mathrm{delay}})_+$. Under \emph{separable} temporal decay $\Sigma_{0,\ell} = \rho(\ell)\Sigma_{0,0}$ with $\Sigma_{\ell,\ell} = \Sigma_{0,0}$ and $|\rho(\ell)| < 1$, a delay-agnostic plug-in $P_{\mathrm{plug}}$ ($\rho \equiv 1$) satisfies $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}(P_{\mathrm{joint}}) = |\rho(\ell)|\,\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}(P_{\mathrm{plug}})$ and over-computes whenever $\IG(P_{\mathrm{joint}}) < \Delta_{\mathrm{delay}} < \IG(P_{\mathrm{plug}})$ (non-separable covariances admit reverse examples).
\item \textbf{Cross-action correlation:} On direct delayed queries $Q = \{(1,\ell),(2,\ell)\}$, as $R_B \to 0$ ($B \to \infty$), $P_{\mathrm{fact}}$ overstates $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}$ (over-computes) iff $\Sigma_{(1,\ell),(2,\ell)} > 0$ \citep{xie2016pairwise}, whereas positively correlated paired noise can reverse the ordering at small $B$. For a single-action query $Q = \{(1,\ell)\}$ with noise variance $r_1$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}(P_{\mathrm{joint}}) = |\Sigma_{11} - \Sigma_{12}|/\sqrt{\Sigma_{11}+r_1}$, so $P_{\mathrm{fact}}$ \emph{over-computes} iff $0 < \Sigma_{12} < 2\Sigma_{11}$ and \emph{under-computes} when $\Sigma_{12} < 0$ or $\Sigma_{12} > 2\Sigma_{11}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Excess-Risk Identity and Target Structural Bounds}

Under the tie-safe rule ``select $a_1$ iff $\widehat{\Delta}_\ell(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_B; P) < 0$'' and $d(P) = \mathbf{1}\{\VoC(P) > 0\}$, any mean-matched belief $\widehat{P}$ satisfies the exact plug-in excess-risk identity under $P_{\mathrm{joint}}$:
\begin{align}\label{eq:regret-decomp}
\mathcal{R}(\widehat{P}; P_{\mathrm{joint}})
&= \big|d(\widehat{P}) - d(P_{\mathrm{joint}})\big|\cdot\big|\VoC(P_{\mathrm{joint}})\big| \nonumber\\[-2pt]
&\quad + d(\widehat{P})\,\E_{P_{\mathrm{joint}}}\!\Big[\big|\widehat{\Delta}_\ell(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_B; P_{\mathrm{joint}})\big|\,\mathbf{1}\!\big\{\mathbf{1}\{\widehat{\Delta}_\ell(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_B; \widehat{P}) < 0\} \ne \mathbf{1}\{\widehat{\Delta}_\ell(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_B; P_{\mathrm{joint}}) < 0\}\big\}\Big].
\end{align}
Building on \eqref{eq:regret-decomp}, our theoretical work targets two non-trivial analytical results:
(i) \textbf{Filter-weight perturbation bounds} upper-bounding post-verification selection regret (the second term in \eqref{eq:regret-decomp}) explicitly in terms of $\|\mathbf{w}(\widehat{P}) - \mathbf{w}(P_{\mathrm{joint}})\|$ and SVI covariance error $\|\widehat{\Sigma}(x) - \Sigma(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}}$; and
(ii) \textbf{Queue-memory and decorrelating stopping bounds}, coupling $\pi_0$ trajectories with queue-clearing time $\tau_{\mathrm{couple}}$ to establish $\IG(\ell) \le \frac{2M}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Pr(\tau_{\mathrm{couple}} > \ell)$ for bounded payoffs $M$, and proving monotonicity of the continuous-time stopping boundary under OU target decorrelation $e^{-t/\vartheta}$ (recovering \citealp{chick2012sequential} as $\vartheta \to \infty$).

\section{Empirical Benchmark, Pilot Diagnostics, and Pre-Registered Gate}

\textbf{Data provenance and pilot diagnostics.}
We use the RESCO benchmark framework \citep{ault2021reinforcement} with the \texttt{saltlake2\_400sX200w} corridor added in RESCO v2.0 \citep{resco2025v2} (sites \texttt{7241}/\texttt{7242}, 730 daily CSVs of 2023 five-minute Utah DOT ATSPM counts). Screening 07:00--08:00 retains \textbf{217 train, 29 validation, 11 eligible dates in the 2023-10-01--10-15 development buffer, and 72 held-out test dates}. Initial native pilot diagnostics on \texttt{2023-10-04} and \texttt{10-05} ($\times \{+300, +900\,\mathrm{s}\}$, 435 states, 12 paired closed-loop units) established three design facts: (a) evaluation horizon $T \ge 60\,\mathrm{s}$ is essential ($30\,\mathrm{s}$ rollouts correlate at $0.33$--$0.38$ with $60\,\mathrm{s}$ truth and lose $-0.34\%$, whereas precomputed $60\,\mathrm{s}$ verification with $B=4$ beats \texttt{greedy5} by $+1.53\%$ TTS, winning $12/12$ dev units); (b) off-grid post-epoch actuation forfeits candidates to MaxPressure in $57.5\%$ of states ($-3.39\%$ TTS) and produces $36.8\%$--$89\%$ structural zeros, motivating our mask $\mathcal{M}_i$; and (c) single-corridor $B=4$ runtime is $\approx 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ inside a $5\,\mathrm{s}$ interval.

\section{Implementation Plan (Julia)}

All modeling, inference, numerical verification, and benchmark orchestration are structured in a unified \texttt{Julia} package (\texttt{ComputationDelayedMDP.jl}): (1) run the pre-registered information-age gate and Tier~1 $\E[y_{irj}]=0$ audit; (2) implement masked heteroscedastic Gibbs and full-covariance SVI with held-out scoring and zero/legality PPCs; (3) complete proofs of the perturbation and queue-memory bounds; and (4) execute the locked 72-date SUMO evaluation, reporting both positive gains and any mechanistic null contrasts transparently.
